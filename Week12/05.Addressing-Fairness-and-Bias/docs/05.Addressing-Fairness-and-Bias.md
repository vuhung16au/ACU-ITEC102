# Algorithmic Fairness & The 80% Rule

When predictive models allocate critical resources (jobs, loans, scholarships, medical interventions), uncritical reliance on naive accuracy can perpetuate historical discrimination.

---

## 1. The Disparate Impact Ratio (DIR) & The 80% Rule

Originally established in US employment law (EEOC Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures) and adopted globally in algorithmic auditing:

$$\text{Selection Rate } (SR) = \frac{\text{Number of Positive Outcomes in Cohort}}{\text{Total Applicants in Cohort}}$$

$$\text{Disparate Impact Ratio } (DIR) = \frac{SR_{\text{unprivileged}}}{SR_{\text{privileged}}}$$

| DIR Value | Interpretation | Legal & Ethical Standing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\text{DIR} \ge 0.80$ | Equal Opportunity / Substantial Parity | **Pass**: Compliant with standard fairness benchmarks |
| $\text{DIR} < 0.80$ | Adverse Impact | **Violation**: Statistically significant disadvantage requiring mitigation |

---

## 2. Taxonomies of Bias

1. **Historical Bias**: The world as it was is biased; algorithms trained on past data naturally learn and automate past inequalities.
2. **Representation Bias**: Minority populations are underrepresented in training samples, causing algorithms to perform with lower fidelity.
3. **Proxy Bias**: Even if protected attributes (gender, race) are excluded, correlated proxies (postcode, sports played, club memberships) leak sensitive information.

---

## 3. Mitigation Strategies

- **Pre-Processing**: Re-sampling or re-weighting biased training distributions.
- **In-Processing**: Adding fairness penalties or adversarial debiasing layers during model training.
- **Post-Processing**: Threshold adjustment (group-specific calibration) to guarantee demographic parity at decision time.
