# Ethics in Data Science: Principles & Frameworks

As data science systems directly shape access to loans, education, employment, and healthcare, data scientists have a professional and legal duty to design transparent, fair, and accountable systems.

---

## 1. The 8 Australian AI Ethics Principles

Developed by the Australian Government (DISR & CSIRO's Data61):

1. **Human, societal and environmental wellbeing**: Systems should benefit individuals, society, and the environment.
2. **Human-centred values**: Systems must respect human rights, diversity, and the autonomy of individuals.
3. **Fairness**: Systems should not lead to unfair discrimination against individuals or communities.
4. **Privacy protection and governance**: Systems must uphold privacy rights and ensure secure data management under the Privacy Act 1988.
5. **Contestability**: Where an AI decision significantly impacts a person, there must be a timely process to challenge the outcome.
6. **Transparency and explainability**: Stakeholders should be aware when AI is used and understand why an outcome was reached.
7. **Robustness, safety and security**: Systems must reliably operate in accordance with their intended purpose.
8. **Accountability**: Human organizations and developers remain responsible for the proper functioning of the system.

---

## 2. Proxy Discrimination

Removing sensitive columns (such as race, gender, or religion) is insufficient if correlated proxy variables remain. For example, using postcode or school attended can replicate historical discrimination. Data scientists must actively test for statistical correlations between protected classes and model inputs.

---

## 3. Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Guardrails

For high-impact decisions (e.g. course suspension, loan denial, criminal sentencing recommendations), systems must not be fully automated. Automated scores must serve as advisory recommendations triaged by human subject-matter experts.
